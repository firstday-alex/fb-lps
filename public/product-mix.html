Product mix, period over period

Reads Shopify orders and their line items for a date range and a comparison period, then reports each product's units, its share of all units sold, and the part of the AOV change it is responsible for. Leave the UTM filters blank for the whole store, or scope them to one channel.

Both filters match the order's first visit exactly and accept a comma-separated list (paid_social,paid), because Meta traffic does not all arrive under one medium.

An order's channel only exists on the order, so every order in both windows has to be read to filter it — about 15 seconds per day of store orders, whatever the UTM filter. A day against a day lands in well under a minute; a week against a week is a couple of minutes. The counter below tracks it.

Units sold by product  (raw change and share of units)

Δ Units is the raw change in units sold in this scope. Share is that product's percentage of every unit sold in the scope, so Δ Share (in percentage points) says whether the mix moved toward it or away from it — a product can lose units and still gain share when the whole scope shrank. Units/Order is units ÷ orders (not ÷ orders containing it), which is the figure that feeds AOV. Attach is the share of orders that contained it at all, so you can tell "fewer baskets bought it" from "the baskets that bought it took fewer".

What each product did to AOV

AOV is exactly the sum of what every product contributes per order: $/Order = that product's net revenue ÷ all orders in scope = Units/Order × ASP. So Δ $/Order is the product's contribution to Δ AOV in dollars, and the rows add up to it. The contribution splits into Units Effect = ΔU/Order × prevASP (selling more or fewer of it at the old price), ASP Effect = prevU/Order × ΔASP (the same quantity at a different net price), and Interaction = ΔU/Order × ΔASP. A product that sold in only one of the two periods shows — for the missing ASP and books its whole move as a units effect, rather than pretending its price fell to zero. ASP here is net — after every discount — and Δ Discount is the change in discount depth (1 − net ÷ list), which is usually what an ASP move actually is. The shipping, tax & other order charges row is the rest of the order total, so the footer ties to the real Δ AOV in the KPI above.

Scope is read from each order's customerJourneySummary.firstVisit, the same first-touch attribution the AOV and CVR tabs use, so order counts and AOV match those tabs exactly and will not tie to Ads Manager. Units are line-item quantity — the same thing subtotalLineItemsQuantity counts, verified equal on a full day — so a free gift-with-purchase counts as a unit at $0, and an order-protection add-on counts as a unit too; both show up as their own rows, and the product-type chips above can take them out of the mix. Product revenue is discountedUnitPriceAfterAllDiscountsSet × quantity: net of line-level and order-level discounts, which at this store are most of the price. It reconciles to each order's subtotal to the cent. List price is originalTotalSet. Everything else in the order total — shipping, tax, tips — is the residual row, never spread across products. A product deleted since the order keeps the name the line item recorded.
